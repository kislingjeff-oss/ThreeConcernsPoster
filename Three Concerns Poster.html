Why now?

Each of my three concerns about Friends is setting records at the same time. These are not abstractions. They are what the three concerns look like this year.

Every concern at once. Hunger, wealth at the top, homelessness and detention are all at or near records together, and they feed each other.
The measuring is being taken away. USDA ended its food security report after the 2024 numbers. What is no longer counted is easier to ignore.
The window is closing. At the 2024 rate, the carbon budget for 1.5°C runs out in about three years from the start of 2025.
Channels take years to build. The relationships that let a community meet its own needs cannot be started on the day they are needed. The moment they are needed is not the moment to begin.

Three concerns, three kinds of extraction

1. The poverty around us

Taken: food and goods, held behind a wall of money. Food sits in the store while people go without.

Hidden by: charity, which manages need.

Answered by: mutual aid, meeting needs as equals.

Why now

In 2024, 13.7% of households could not count on food, the most since 2014, and the report that counted them has been ended. Homelessness reached a record. Need is growing while the means of seeing it are being taken away. Generations of charity have not ended it. Each year spent carrying buckets leaves the river diverted upstream.

Why doing nothing is not a Quaker response

"If a brother or sister be naked, and destitute of daily food, and one of you say unto them, Depart in peace, be ye warmed and filled; notwithstanding ye give them not those things which are needful to the body; what doth it profit?" (James 2:15–16). Our own Advices say each of us is both giver and receiver. Doing nothing is not neutral. The arrangement keeps running, and we keep living on it.

Do we recognize the difference between charity and mutual aid? Do we understand how it shows the fundamental differences in hierarchy and power each represents?

2. Who gets to be free

Taken: land and labor, and young people's futures, through the economic draft.

Hidden by: deeds and "help."

Answered by: LANDBACK, and showing up to work by invitation.

Why now

The top 1% now hold a record share of household wealth. Debt, job lock and enlistment are deciding young people's lives before they can choose. At the 2024 rate, the carbon budget for 1.5°C runs out in about three years from the start of 2025, so young people's futures are being spent now. And there are channels ready to join: in 2025 Great Plains Action Society secured land for its Urban Resilience Hub in Iowa City, and its work-weeks welcome people who will show up.

Why doing nothing is not a Quaker response

Woolman wrote that a claim proved by seals and witnesses does not become right: "the nature of it must be changed before it can be right." In 1874 the committee on Indian affairs of my own meeting reported that it "found nothing to do." We are still living with what that inaction left in place. We cannot hold a testimony of equality while still occupying, unexamined, the position that inequality built for us.

We know there are millions suffering an economic draft: from student debt, from needing a job because of its health benefits, or from a lack of economic opportunity that leads to the armed forces.

3. The violence holding it up

Taken: safety, by the force that keeps the taking in place.

Hidden by: neutrality.

Answered by: active objection, and standing with people where the force falls.

Why now

About 73,000 people were held in ICE detention in January 2026, the most ever recorded and up about 84% in a year. Neighbors are being taken. Force grows fastest where the people nearby look away, and every month it grows, it becomes more normal.

Why doing nothing is not a Quaker response

Our peace testimony was never only a refusal to fight. George Fox said he lived "in the virtue of that life and power that took away the occasion of all wars." Friends in my own meeting refused the draft during the Vietnam War, at real cost. Quaker neutrality in front of armed enforcement isn't peace. It leaves the force free to fall on others while we keep our own hands clean.

The scenes of armed repression and deportation are horrifying to watch. We know where this is likely to go. How can we say we are a peaceful people if we simply watch?

What a new direction asks

Adjustment is not repair. A gentler way of taking is still taking. The new direction is a turn away from one-way channels altogether, and it can begin now, in our own lives.

  1. Name the channel. Say plainly which way it flows, in each case.
  2. Withdraw where you really can. Hold less that ties your welfare to someone else's loss.
  3. Open or join a channel that runs both ways. Mutual aid, LANDBACK, showing up for work, by invitation, and staying.

It matters little what we say we believe while our lives still run on the arrangement that produces the suffering we say we oppose.

From Three Concerns About Friends, Expanded Edition, by Jeff Kisling, member of Bear Creek Meeting, Iowa Yearly Meeting (Conservative). Speaks for no meeting or organization.
